Low-end PCs

Fiordland on old and low-end PCs

Fiordland is meant for a ten-year-old laptop as much as a new gaming desktop. The same install adapts to the machine it lands on.

What happens on a weak machine

  • A Low-end PC switch in the installer. It leaves out the gaming packages and 32-bit graphics drivers, and points you to the light desktops.
  • Automatic tuning. fiordland-tune checks the hardware at boot. With less than 4 GB of memory, two CPU threads or no GPU acceleration it picks the low-end profile: zram swap as large as memory with zstd compression, small write-back buffers so the disk never stalls the desktop, and no proactive memory compaction.
  • Runs on any 64-bit CPU. Packages are Arch's generic x86-64 builds and the Fiordland kernel targets the x86-64 baseline, so CPUs without AVX2 are fine.
  • A responsive kernel. The Fiordland kernel uses the BORE scheduler, a 1000 Hz timer and full preemption, and keeps transparent huge pages on madvise so 4 to 8 GB machines don't waste memory.
  • Out-of-memory protection. systemd-oomd stops a runaway app before the whole system freezes.

Light desktops

Full desktops that stay light: Xfce, LXQt, MATE and LXDE. Lighter still: Openbox, IceWM, Fluxbox, i3, labwc and Sway. You can switch later with weka, without reinstalling.

Change it by hand

sudo fiordland-tune apply low-end, balanced or performance picks a profile, sudo fiordland-tune apply auto goes back to detecting it, and fiordland-tune status shows what is in use.

Requirements

  • Any 64-bit x86 PC.
  • 4 GB of RAM recommended, 8 GB or more is better.
  • 30 GB of disk.
